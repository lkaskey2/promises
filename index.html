Our Promises

Things we strive for, every day.

The promiseIf it slips
  1. Assume love.Whatever we hear from each other, whatever the tone or words, it comes from love.
    Ask, don't assume: “Can I check what you meant?” The other answers plainly.
  2. No relitigating.We raise things without dragging up old arguments or hurts.
    Either of us can say “That's from before.” We come back to the current topic.
  3. Think positively.We choose positive thoughts about each other and about us, every day.
    Tell the other one specific thing you appreciate about them that day.
  4. Keep the spark.We each plan dates and special moments, at least monthly.
    Whoever missed their month plans the next date within two weeks.
  5. State of the Union.Wednesdays at 9 PM, 15–30 minutes, to talk through the past week.
    Reschedule within 48 hours. We never skip two weeks in a row.
  6. Keep merging.We're building one life together, emotionally and financially, with no deadline.
    Raise it at the next State of the Union with a soft start.
  7. Bookend the day.Good morning and good night texts, daily.
    Send a quick “missed you” text as soon as you notice. No guilt either way.
  8. Say it sweetly.BABE, amor, love: often.
    A light reminder is fine: “Call me BABE.” Taken with a smile.
  9. Us first.We're each other's default priority, with understood exceptions.
    The one who stepped away acknowledges it. If it repeats, it goes to State of the Union.
  10. Kid time matters.We help each other make the most of time with our kids as they continue to build their own lives.
    A quick reminder that we're just trying to make the most of this time.
  11. Freedom to go.Liz can visit her kids or do any activities with her kids without guilt. Larry can do the same. Period.
    If guilt shows up, either of us says “Promise 11,” and we let it go.
  12. Travel together.One 5+ day vacation a year, minimum.
    Put dates on the calendar together within a month.
  13. Heart before head.Compassion and empathy come before being right, fixing, or blaming.
    Pause and say “Let me start over.” Lead with empathy the second time.
  14. De-escalate.We keep finding ways to cool things down. An apology isn't admitting blame. It's a good way to start.
    Take at least a 20-minute break, then come back. Either of us can apologize first.
  15. One topic at a time.When one of us raises something, the other responds to that, not with “you did it too.” If you have your own concern, it gets its own turn.
    Either of us says “One at a time.” The other concern waits for its own turn, usually at State of the Union.

State of the Union

Wednesdays · 9 PM15–30 minutes

If one of us is away, we do it by phone or video.

Rules

  • Only the past week counts.
  • Phones down.
  • We take turns speaking and listening.

Agenda

  1. One issue each. The Speaker shares how they feel, without placing blame. The Listener tries to understand, not defend.
  2. One wish each. “One thing that would help me feel loved this week is…” Be specific, and ask for more of something rather than for someone to stop something.

Soft start

“I just want to tell you how I feel, not place blame.”
I feel [emotion]
about [a specific situation]
I need [something positive]
“What do you think?”

Keep it a complaint, not criticism: no “you always,” “you never,” or attacks on character.

Listening

  • Two different views can both be valid.
  • Respond with empathy.
  • Don't answer an issue with one of your own.

Sources

  • Couples Therapy Inc. – Softened Start-Up
  • Insights Counseling – Criticism vs. Complaints
